## Auditable future workflow — design only

1. **Appraise and map.** Custodian identifies the official copy and related copies/derived records. Records Officer maps the approved series/trigger/period and archival requirements. DPO confirms purpose, minimization, lawful restrictions and rights handling. Unclassified records remain under review.
2. **Prepare a read-only manifest.** Record target identifiers/versions, case/dependency links, bucket/object versions, derivative versions, proposed trigger/effective date, hold-register result and backup/recipient obligations. Store only the minimum necessary restricted metadata. Calculate a manifest hash and record the inventory/hold revision.
3. **Review preservation and holds.** Competent officers resolve active/reopened cases, complaints, incidents and preservation/transfer needs. Missing data or authority blocks progression. Issuing/releasing a hold must be independently authorized, scoped and audited; a user-supplied boolean or date cannot clear it.
4. **Obtain required scoped written authority.** Follow the approved provincial/records procedure and applicable NAP process. Record verified authority reference, issuer/delegation, covered series/dates/manifest, method, conditions, witnesses and validity limits. Requester cannot self-approve. DPO, Records Officer and external records authority have distinct functions; an application admin cannot replace them.
5. **Mark approved for disposition only after verification.** A future server-side state transition must bind authenticated authorized approvers to the exact manifest/version and required authority. The state does not execute anything, and approvals can be withdrawn/expire. This milestone does not persist or authorize that transition.
6. **Require separate implementation and execution approval.** No execution exists in this prototype. A later concrete executor/change must be separately authorized after the policy/procedure exists. At execution, recheck identity/MFA/delegation, policy version, approval validity, hold/dependency revisions and manifest content under concurrency controls. Any drift returns the batch to review.
7. **Execute and reconcile only within that later authority.** Use a scoped, idempotent plan with per-store receipts. Failures keep the batch incomplete/restricted; never mark a failed Storage/provider operation successful. Preserve specifically required case/audit evidence, not disposed text. Report recipient or provider limitations honestly.
8. **Verify, certify and protect recovery.** A separately authorized reviewer/witness checks the required completion evidence and disposition certificate. Preserve a minimal audit of request, review, authority, manifest hash, scope/counts, execution outcome and exceptions. Replay authorized restrictions/disposition decisions after any restore before access resumes.

A future approval ledger should append corrections/revocations rather than overwriting signed decisions. It must capture actor identity/role/delegation, current session assurance, timestamp, event, reason code, policy/series/manifest versions and supporting authority reference. Keep private legal reasons and document access separately restricted. Never include tokens, PDF bytes or employment free text in routine decision logs. A protected copy of the ledger must survive restoration of an older application database. This is a design requirement, not an installed immutable audit system.
